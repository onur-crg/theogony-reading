p057 (901–917): Next Zeus married radiant Themis. She bore the Seasons: Eunomia, Dike, and flourishing Eirene, who watch over the work of mortal people. She also bore the Fates, whom Zeus the wise planner honored above all: Clotho, Lachesis, and Atropos, who give mortal people their share of good and bad.

Eurynome, the lovely daughter of Oceanus, bore Zeus three Graces with beautiful cheeks: Aglaia, Euphrosyne, and lovely Thalia. From their eyes, as they looked, flowed desire that melts the limbs. Beautiful were their glances beneath their brows.

Then Zeus lay with Demeter, who feeds so many. She bore white-armed Persephone, whom Aidoneus snatched away from her mother. Zeus the wise planner gave her to him.

Then Zeus fell in love with lovely-haired Mnemosyne. She bore him the nine Muses with golden headbands, who delight in feasts and the joy of song.

p058 (918–929): Leto joined in love with Zeus, who bears the aegis, and gave birth to Apollo and Artemis the archer—children lovelier than all the other gods of heaven.

Last of all, Zeus made Hera in her full bloom his wife. She joined in love with the king of gods and humans and bore Hebe, Ares, and Eileithyia.

Zeus himself brought forth bright-eyed Tritogeneia from his own head: Athena, the fearsome, tireless goddess who stirs up battle and leads armies. She delights in the clash of fighting, in wars and battles.

Hera, furious with her husband and quarreling with him, gave birth to famous Hephaestus without joining anyone in love. He surpassed all the gods of heaven in craftsmanship.

p059 (930–946): From Amphitrite and the thundering Earth-shaker Poseidon came great Triton, mighty in strength. This fearsome god lives in a golden house at the bottom of the sea, beside his dear mother and his father the king.

Cytherea bore Phobos and Deimos to Ares, who pierces shields. These terrifying sons throw tightly packed ranks of warriors into confusion in chilling war, alongside Ares the city-sacker. She also bore Harmonia, whom bold Cadmus made his wife.

Maia, daughter of Atlas, entered Zeus's sacred bed and bore him glorious Hermes, messenger of the immortals.

Semele, daughter of Cadmus, joined Zeus in love and bore him a splendid son, Dionysus, full of joy. She was mortal; her son was immortal. Now both are gods.

Alcmene joined in love with Zeus the cloud-gatherer and gave birth to mighty Heracles.

And Hephaestus, the famous god with disabled legs, made flourishing Aglaia, the youngest of the Graces, his wife.

p060 (947–962): Golden-haired Dionysus made fair-haired Ariadne, daughter of Minos, his wife in her full bloom. For his sake, Zeus son of Cronus made her immortal and free from aging.

After completing his painful labors, mighty Heracles, the brave son of Alcmene with her lovely ankles, made honored Hebe his wife on snowy Olympus. She is the daughter of great Zeus and Hera of the golden sandals. Happy Heracles! His great work accomplished, he lives among the immortals, free from harm and aging for all time.

The renowned Perseis, a daughter of Oceanus, bore Circe and King Aeetes to tireless Helios. Aeetes, son of Helios who shines on mortals, married beautiful-cheeked Idyia, a daughter of Oceanus, the river that circles the world. This marriage followed the gods' wishes. Through golden Aphrodite's power, Idyia yielded to Aeetes in love and bore him Medea with her lovely ankles.
