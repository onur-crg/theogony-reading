p047 (711–728): Then the battle turned. Until then, the two sides had held their ground against each other, fighting fiercely without giving way.

At the front, Cottus, Briareus, and Gyges, who could never get enough of war, stirred up savage fighting. From their powerful hands they hurled three hundred rocks, one after another, until their missiles overshadowed the Titans. They overpowered those proud gods with their hands, sent them beneath the earth with its wide roads, and bound them in painful chains—as far beneath the earth as the sky is above it. That is how far it is from earth to misty Tartarus.

A bronze anvil falling from the sky for nine nights and nine days would reach the earth on the tenth day. And a bronze anvil falling from earth for nine nights and nine days would reach Tartarus on the tenth.

A bronze fence has been built around Tartarus. Night spreads in three layers around its neck, while above it grow the roots of earth and the sea that yields no harvest.

p048 (729–744): There the Titan gods are hidden in misty darkness, by the will of Zeus the cloud-gatherer, in a dank place at the ends of the vast earth. They cannot get out. Poseidon has fitted bronze doors across the entrance, and a wall runs around on either side. There Gyges, Cottus, and great-hearted Obriareus live as faithful guards of Zeus, who carries the aegis.

There, side by side, lie the sources and boundaries of dark earth, misty Tartarus, the sea that yields no harvest, and the starry sky—dreadful, dank places that even the gods hate.

There is a huge chasm. Once inside its gates, you would not reach the bottom even in a whole year. Instead, one savage storm after another would carry you this way and that. Even the immortal gods find this marvel terrifying.

There too is the fearsome house of dark Night.

p049 (745–766): Night’s house stands wrapped in blue-black clouds. Before it, Iapetus’s son stands firm, holding up the broad sky with his head and tireless hands.

Here Night and Day draw near and greet each other as they cross the great bronze threshold. One goes inside while the other goes out; the house never holds them both at once. One is always outside, travelling over the earth, while the other waits inside until her own time to set out arrives. Day brings people on earth the light that lets them see far. Deadly Night, wrapped in a misty cloud, carries Sleep in her hands, the brother of Death.

Here dark Night’s children have their homes: Sleep and Death, fearsome gods. Shining Helios never looks upon them with his rays, either as he climbs into the sky or as he comes down from it.

Sleep travels peacefully over the earth and the broad back of the sea, gentle toward human beings. But Death has a heart of iron, a merciless heart of bronze inside his chest. Once he seizes someone, he holds on. Even the immortal gods hate him.

p050 (767–774): There, in front, stand the echoing halls of the god below, mighty Hades, and dreaded Persephone. A fearsome, merciless dog keeps watch before them. He has a cruel trick: he greets everyone coming in with his wagging tail and both ears, but he never lets them go back out. He lies in wait and devours anyone he catches going outside the gates.
