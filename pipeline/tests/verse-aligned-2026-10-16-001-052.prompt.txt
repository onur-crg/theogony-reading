EARLIER ENGLISH DRAFT
p001 (1–8): Let us begin our song with the Muses, goddesses of song,
who live on Helicon, the great and sacred mountain.
On soft feet they dance around the violet-dark spring
and the altar of mighty Zeus, the son of Cronus.
After washing their tender skin in the river Permessus,
or the Horse’s Spring, or the sacred river Olmeius,
they form lovely, graceful dances high on Helicon,
their feet moving with force.

p002 (9–21): From there they set out, wrapped in thick mist,
walking through the night, their beautiful voices rising.
They sing of Zeus, who carries the aegis, his shield of power;
of majestic Hera of Argos, walking in golden sandals;
of bright-eyed Athena, daughter of aegis-bearing Zeus;
of shining Apollo and Artemis, who showers arrows;
of Poseidon, who holds the earth and shakes it;
of honored Themis, goddess of justice, and Aphrodite,
goddess of love, with her curling lashes;
of Hebe, goddess of youth, crowned in gold, and beautiful Dione;
of Leto, Apollo and Artemis’s mother, and the Titan Iapetus;
of Cronus with his crafty plans;
of Eos the Dawn, great Helios the Sun, and bright Selene the Moon;
of Gaia the Earth, great Oceanus, the river around the world,
and black Night, and the sacred family
of all the other gods who live forever.

p003 (22–34): These were the goddesses who once taught Hesiod beautiful song
while he tended lambs below sacred Helicon.
This is what they said to me first—
the Muses of Olympus, daughters of Zeus who carries the aegis:
“Shepherds who live out in the fields, shameful creatures, nothing but bellies!
We know how to tell many lies that sound like the truth;
and we know, when we choose, how to speak the truth.”
So spoke great Zeus’s daughters, their words clear and sure.
They plucked a branch from a flourishing laurel tree
and gave it to me as a staff—a wonderful sight.
Then they breathed a divine voice into me,
so I could sing of things to come and things long past.
They told me to praise the family of blessed gods who live forever,
and always to sing of the Muses themselves, first and last.

p004 (35–52): But why am I talking like this about oak or rock?
Come, let us begin with the Muses.
Inside Olympus, the mountain home of the gods,
their songs delight the great mind of Zeus their father.
Their voices join as they tell of what is,
what will be, and what was before.
Sweet song flows tirelessly from their mouths.
Their father Zeus’s halls laugh with joy—
Zeus whose thunder booms—as the goddesses’ lily-delicate voices spread.
The peak of snowy Olympus echoes,
and so do the homes of the immortal gods.
Sending out their deathless voices,
they first sing in praise of the gods’ honored family from the beginning:
those born to Gaia the Earth and wide Uranus the Sky,
and the gods descended from them, the givers of good things.
Next they sing of Zeus, father of gods and humans,
at both the beginning and the end of their song:
how far he surpasses the gods, how greatest he is in power.
Then they sing of humankind and the mighty Giants,
those powerful beings of the ancient divine world.
So the Muses of Olympus, daughters of aegis-bearing Zeus,
delight their father’s mind inside Olympus.
