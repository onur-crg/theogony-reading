p006 (63–79): There the Muses, the goddesses of song, have shining dancing grounds and beautiful homes. Beside them live the Graces, goddesses of beauty and charm, and Himeros, the god of desire, surrounded by celebration. Lovely voices flow from the Muses’ mouths as they sing of all the immortal gods’ laws and praise their noble ways.

Then they went toward Olympus, the mountain home of the gods, delighting in their beautiful voices, their divine song. The dark earth rang around them as they sang, and a lovely beat rose beneath their feet as they walked toward their father. He is Zeus, king of heaven, who holds the thunder and the smoking lightning bolt. By his strength he defeated his father Cronus, then set everything in good order for the immortal gods and assigned each their honors.

These things the Muses sang, whose homes are on Olympus: nine daughters born to mighty Zeus—Clio, Euterpe, Thalia, Melpomene, Terpsichore, Erato, Polyhymnia, Urania, and Calliope, the foremost of them all.

p007 (80–93): For Calliope also walks beside respected kings. When the Muses, mighty Zeus’s daughters, choose to honor a king sustained by Zeus and watch over him at birth, they pour sweet dew onto his tongue, and gentle words flow from his mouth. All the people look to him as he settles disputes with fair judgments. Speaking with confidence and skill, he can quickly end even a bitter quarrel.

This is why kings are wise: when people have been wronged, they can easily set matters right in the public assembly, winning people over with gentle words. As such a king walks through the gathering, people greet him like a god, with warm respect, and he stands out among the crowd. Such is the Muses’ sacred gift to human beings.

p008 (94–103): From the Muses and Apollo, who shoots from afar,
come singers and lyre players on earth;
from Zeus come kings.
Fortunate is anyone the Muses love:
sweet speech flows from his mouth.

Someone may be wasting away with a wounded heart,
his spirit weighed down by fresh grief.
But when a singer, a servant of the Muses,
sings of the glorious deeds of people long ago
and the blessed gods who live on Olympus,
he soon forgets his sadness and remembers none of his troubles.
So quickly do the goddesses’ gifts turn his thoughts away.

p009 (104–115): Greetings, daughters of Zeus! Give me a song that stirs longing. Celebrate the sacred family of the gods who live forever: those born of Gaia, the Earth, and starry Uranus, the Sky; those born of dark Night; and those whom Pontus, the salty Sea, brought up.

Tell how, in the beginning, the gods and the earth came into being, and the rivers and the boundless sea, surging with waves, and the shining stars and the wide sky above—[and the gods born from these, the givers of good things]. Tell how the gods divided their riches and shared out their honors, and how they first took possession of Olympus with its many ridges.

Tell me these things, Muses whose homes are on Olympus, from the beginning. Tell me which of them came into being first.
