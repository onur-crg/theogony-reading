p061 (963–968): Now farewell, you who live on Olympus—and you islands, mainlands, and the salt sea between them.

Now sing of the goddesses, sweet-voiced Muses of Olympus, daughters of Zeus who carries the aegis: the immortal goddesses who slept with mortal men and bore children like gods.

p062 (969–987): Demeter, radiant among goddesses, joined the hero Iasion in sweet love in a field plowed three times, in the fertile land of Crete. She bore kindly Plutus, who travels everywhere over the earth and the sea’s broad back. Whoever meets him, whoever takes him into their hands, he makes rich and gives great wealth.

Harmonia, daughter of golden Aphrodite, bore Cadmus these children in Thebes with its beautiful crown: Ino, Semele, lovely-cheeked Agave, Autonoe—whom Aristaeus with his flowing hair married—and Polydorus.

Callirhoe, daughter of Oceanus, joined strong-hearted Chrysaor in love through Aphrodite, rich in gold. She bore Geryon, the strongest of all mortals. Mighty Heracles killed him for his cattle with their rolling gait, on Erytheia, surrounded by the sea.

Eos bore Tithonus two sons: Memnon in his bronze helmet, king of the Ethiopians, and lord Emathion. With Cephalus she had another splendid son, mighty Phaethon, a man like the gods.

p063 (988–1002): While Phaethon was still young, in the tender flower of glorious youth, a boy with a carefree heart, smile-loving Aphrodite swept him away. In her most sacred temples she made him her attendant by night, a divine spirit.

By the will of the gods who live forever, Jason, son of Aeson, led Medea, the daughter of King Aeetes, away from her father. Aeetes was a king sustained by Zeus. Jason had completed the many painful trials imposed on him by the great King Pelias—a proud, violent, reckless man whose deeds were brutal.

After completing those trials and suffering greatly, Jason reached Iolcus in his swift ship, bringing the bright-eyed girl with him, and made her his wife in the bloom of her youth. Joined in love with Jason, shepherd of his people, Medea bore a son, Medeius. Chiron, son of Philyra, raised him in the mountains. And so great Zeus’s plan was fulfilled.

p064 (1003–1018): Then there were the daughters of Nereus, the old man of the sea. Psamathe, radiant among goddesses, bore Phocus after joining Aeacus in love through golden Aphrodite. And the silver-footed goddess Thetis joined Peleus and bore Achilles, who broke through men’s ranks and had a lion’s heart.

Cytherea with her beautiful crown bore Aeneas after joining the hero Anchises in sweet love, high on wooded Mount Ida with its many folds.

Circe, daughter of Helios, the son of Hyperion, joined patient-hearted Odysseus in love. She bore Agrius and Latinus, a man both noble and strong. Far, far away, deep among the sacred islands, they ruled over all the famous Tyrrhenians.

Calypso, radiant among goddesses, joined Odysseus in sweet love and bore him Nausithous and Nausinous.

p065 (1019–1022): These were the immortal goddesses who slept with mortal men and bore children like gods.

Now sing of the women, sweet-voiced Muses of Olympus, daughters of Zeus who carries the aegis.
