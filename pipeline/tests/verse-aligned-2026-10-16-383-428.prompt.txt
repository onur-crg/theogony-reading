EARLIER ENGLISH DRAFT
p028 (383–403): Styx, Oceanus’s daughter, joined in love with Pallas
and in their halls gave birth to Zelos and Nike with her lovely ankles.
She also bore Kratos and Bia, her glorious children.
They have no home, no seat away from Zeus,
and take no road unless the god leads them.
They always sit beside Zeus, whose thunder crashes loud.

This was what deathless Styx, Oceanus’s daughter, had planned
on the day when Zeus, the lightning-flinger of Olympus,
called all the immortal gods to high Olympus.
He said that any god who fought beside him against the Titans
would lose none of their privileges:
each would keep the honor they had held among the immortal gods.
And anyone left without honor or privilege under Cronus
would receive both, as was their right.

Deathless Styx was the first to come to Olympus,
bringing her children, following her dear father’s advice.
Zeus honored her and gave her special gifts.
He made her the great oath by which the gods swear,
and gave her children a home beside him for all time.
He fulfilled his promises to all the gods, every one.
He himself holds mighty power and rules.

p029 (404–413): Phoebe came to Coeus’s bed, filled with love.
The goddess conceived in love with the god
and bore Leto in her dark-blue robe,
always gentle, kind to humans and immortal gods,
gentle from the very first, the kindest on Olympus.

Phoebe also bore renowned Asteria,
whom Perses once brought to his great house
to be called his dear wife.
Asteria conceived and gave birth to Hecate,
whom Zeus, Cronus’s son, honored above all others.
He gave her splendid gifts:
a share of the earth and the unharvested sea.

p030 (414–428): Hecate also has a share of honor in starry heaven,
and the immortal gods honor her greatly.
Even now, whenever someone on earth
seeks divine favor with fine offerings, as custom requires,
they call on Hecate.
Great honor comes easily to anyone
whose prayers the goddess welcomes with goodwill.
She gives them prosperity too, for she has the power.

Of all the gods born from Gaia and Uranus
who received a share of honor,
Hecate has a portion of every one’s share.
Zeus, Cronus’s son, neither forced her aside
nor took away anything she had received
among the Titans, the earlier gods.
She keeps her share from that first division.
Though an only child, the goddess has no smaller share
of honor and privilege on earth, in heaven, and at sea,
but far more still, because Zeus honors her.
