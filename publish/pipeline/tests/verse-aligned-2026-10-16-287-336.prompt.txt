EARLIER ENGLISH DRAFT
p022 (287–305): Chrysaor fathered three-headed Geryon
with Callirhoe, daughter of Oceanus,
the famous river god who circles the world.
Mighty Heracles killed Geryon
beside his cattle with their rolling walk,
on Erytheia, an island surrounded by water.
That day he drove the broad-browed cattle
to the sacred city of Tiryns,
crossing the stream of Oceanus,
after killing Orthus, the watchdog,
and Eurytion, the cattle herder,
in their misty cattle shelter beyond famous Oceanus.

She also bore another monster, impossible to overcome,
nothing like mortal humans or the deathless gods:
fierce-hearted, divine Echidna, born in a hollow cave.
Half of her was a nymph, a female nature spirit,
with lovely cheeks and lively eyes;
the other half was a monstrous snake, huge and dreadful,
with a dappled skin, eating flesh raw
in the hidden depths of the sacred earth.
Her cave lies beneath a hollow rock,
far from deathless gods and mortal humans.
There the gods assigned her a splendid home.
There dreadful Echidna keeps watch underground,
in the land of the Arimoi—
a nymph who never dies or grows old, through all her days.

p023 (306–336): They say Typhaon, a monstrous being,
terrible, violent, and lawless,
joined in love with that bright-eyed girl, Echidna.
She became pregnant and bore fierce-hearted children.

First she bore Orthus, the dog who served Geryon.
Then came Cerberus, a monster beyond description,
impossible to overcome: the raw-flesh-eating dog
of Hades, god of the dead,
with a voice like bronze and fifty heads,
merciless and strong.
Third she bore the Hydra of Lerna,
a water monster skilled in deadly harm.
The white-armed goddess Hera raised her,
in boundless anger at mighty Heracles.
But Heracles, son of Zeus, the chief god,
and known as Amphitryon’s son,
killed her with pitiless bronze,
helped by Iolaus, who loved battle,
and guided by Athena, goddess of battle and its spoils.

She also bore the Chimera, breathing unstoppable fire,
terrible and huge, swift-footed and strong.
She had three heads: one a fierce-eyed lion’s,
one a goat’s, and one a snake’s—a mighty serpent’s.
Pegasus, the winged horse, and brave Bellerophon
brought her down.

After mating with Orthus, she bore the deadly Sphinx,
a monster that brought ruin to the people of Thebes,
and the lion of Nemea.
Hera, Zeus’s glorious wife, raised that lion
and settled him in Nemea’s hills, a curse on humans.
Living there, he ravaged the local people,
ruling over Tretus, Nemea, and Apesas.
But mighty Heracles used his strength to bring him down.

Ceto, a sea goddess, joined in love with Phorcys,
a sea god, and bore her youngest child:
a dreadful snake that guards the all-golden apples
in the dark earth’s hidden places, at its vast outer limits.
Such are the offspring of Ceto and Phorcys.
