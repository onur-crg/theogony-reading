EARLIER ENGLISH DRAFT
p010 (116–125): First of all, Chaos came into being—a vast gap. Then came Gaia, broad-chested Earth, the firm seat of all things forever; and misty Tartarus, deep in the earth with its wide paths; and Eros, the god of desire, most beautiful of the immortal gods. He loosens limbs and overpowers the mind and careful judgment in the hearts of every god and every human.

From Chaos came Erebus, the darkness, and black Night. From Night came Aether, the bright upper air, and Day. She conceived and gave birth to them after joining with Erebus in love.

p011 (126–138): First Gaia, Earth, gave birth to starry Uranus, Sky, equal to herself in size, to cover her on every side and be a firm seat forever for the blessed gods.

She gave birth to the tall Mountains, lovely homes for the Nymphs—the goddesses who live among the mountains’ valleys. She also bore Pontus, the barren Sea, raging with waves, without joining anyone in the pleasures of love.

Then she lay with Sky and bore Oceanus, the great river with its deep whirlpools; Coeus, Crius, Hyperion, and Iapetus; Theia, Rhea, Themis, and Mnemosyne; Phoebe with her golden crown, and lovely Tethys.

After them came the youngest, Cronus, with his crooked schemes—the most fearsome of her children. He hated his vigorous father.

p012 (139–153): Earth also gave birth to the Cyclopes, with their fierce hearts: Brontes, Steropes, and mighty-hearted Arges. They gave Zeus, the thunder god, his thunder and made his thunderbolt.

In every other way they were like the gods, but a single eye lay in the middle of each forehead. They were called Cyclopes—“Round-eyes”—because each had that one round eye in his forehead. Strength, force, and skill showed in everything they made.

Three more children were born to Earth and Sky, huge and powerful, too terrible to name: Cottus, Briareus, and Gyges, towering sons. A hundred monstrous hands sprang from each one’s shoulders, and fifty heads grew from the shoulders above each one’s sturdy limbs. Their enormous bodies held boundless, mighty strength.

p013 (154–173): All the children born to Earth and Sky were fearsome indeed, and their father hated them from the start. As soon as each was born, Sky hid them away in a hollow inside Earth and let none come into the light. He took pleasure in this cruel work.

Huge Earth groaned inside, cramped by the burden. She thought up a cunning, harmful plan. Quickly she created a substance, gray adamant—a material of extraordinary hardness—and made a great sickle, a curved cutting blade. Then she explained her plan to her dear children. With an aching heart, she spoke to give them courage:

“My children, yours is a reckless father. If you are willing to listen to me, we could make him pay for his cruel abuse. He was the first to plot these shameful deeds.”

So she spoke. Fear seized them all, and none said a word. But great Cronus, with his crooked schemes, took courage and answered his loving mother:

“Mother, I promise I can carry out this deed. I have no concern for our father, with his shameful name. He was the first to plot these shameful deeds.”

So he spoke, and huge Earth’s heart filled with joy.
