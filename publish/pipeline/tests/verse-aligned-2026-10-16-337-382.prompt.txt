p024 (337–345): Tethys bore Ocean the swirling Rivers:
Nile, Alpheus, and Eridanus with his deep whirlpools;
Strymon, Maeander, and Ister with his lovely streams;
Phasis, Rhesus, and Achelous with his silver swirls;
Nessus, Rhodius, Haliacmon, and Heptaporus;
Grenicus, Aesepus, and sacred Simois;
Peneus, Hermus, and Caicus with his fine-flowing waters;
great Sangarius, Ladon, and Parthenius;
Evenus, Ardescus, and sacred Scamander.

p025 (346–370): Tethys also bore a sacred family of daughters.
Across the earth, they nurture the young,
together with Lord Apollo and the Rivers:
this is the task Zeus has given them.

They are Peitho, Admete, Ianthe, Electra,
Doris, Prymno, and godlike Urania;
Hippo, Clymene, Rhodea, Callirhoe,
Zeuxo, Clytie, Idyia, Pasithoe,
Plexaura, Galaxaura, and lovely Dione;
Melobosis, Thoe, and beautiful Polydora;
Cerceis, lovely in form, and wide-eyed Pluto;
Perseis, Ianeira, Acaste, Xanthe,
lovely Petraea, Menestho, Europa,
Metis, Eurynome, and Telesto in her saffron-colored robe;
Chryseis, Asia, and enchanting Calypso;
Eudora, Tyche, Amphiro, Ocyroe,
and Styx, the foremost of them all.

These are the eldest daughters born to Ocean and Tethys,
but there are many others.
There are three thousand slender-ankled daughters of Ocean,
scattered far and wide, watching over the earth
and the depths of the waters everywhere alike—
glorious children of goddesses.

And there are just as many other Rivers,
sons of Ocean whom the noble Tethys bore,
whose waters rush and roar.
It would be hard for a mortal to tell all their names,
but people living beside each river know its name.

p026 (371–374): Theia, yielding to Hyperion in love,
bore great Helios, the Sun, and bright Selene, the Moon,
and Eos, the Dawn, who shines for everyone on earth
and for the immortal gods who live in the wide sky.

p027 (375–382): Eurybia, shining among goddesses,
joined Crius in love and bore him
great Astraeus, Pallas, and Perses,
whose knowledge set him apart from everyone.

Eos lay with Astraeus, goddess with god,
and bore him the fierce-hearted Winds:
Zephyrus, who clears the sky, swift-moving Boreas,
and Notus.

After the Winds, Eos, born at daybreak,
bore Eosphorus, the Morning Star,
and the gleaming stars that crown the sky.
