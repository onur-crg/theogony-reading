p044 (654–663): So Zeus spoke, and noble Cottus answered:

“Mighty god, what you tell us is no mystery. We know for ourselves how far your wisdom and understanding reach beyond all others. You have shielded the immortal gods from chilling ruin. Thanks to your good judgment, we have come back from the misty darkness, free of merciless chains, after suffering beyond imagining, Lord Zeus, son of Cronus. So now, with steady minds and careful planning, we will defend your power in this terrible war, fighting the Titans through the fiercest battles.”

p045 (664–686): So Cottus spoke. The gods, the givers of good things, approved his words, and their hearts longed for war even more than before. That day they all stirred up a dreadful battle, female and male alike: the Titan gods, the children of Cronus, and those Zeus had brought up into the light from Erebus beneath the earth—terrifying, powerful beings with overwhelming strength.

Each of those giants had a hundred hands springing from his shoulders, and fifty heads grew from his shoulders above his sturdy limbs. Now they faced the Titans in deadly battle, gripping towering rocks in their powerful hands. Across from them, the Titans eagerly tightened their battle ranks. Both sides showed what their hands and strength could do.

The boundless sea roared terribly all around. The earth crashed aloud; the wide sky shook and groaned. Tall Olympus shuddered from its base under the immortals’ onslaught. Deep tremors reached misty Tartarus, along with the piercing roar of feet in the endless rush of battle and the crash of mighty blows. So they hurled their weapons of pain at one another. The cries of both sides reached the starry sky as they called out and clashed with a mighty battle shout.

p046 (687–710): Now Zeus no longer held back his fury. At once it filled his heart, and he revealed his full strength. From heaven and Olympus he advanced, flashing lightning without a pause. Thunderbolts flew thick and fast from his powerful hand, with thunder and lightning, whirling sacred flame.

All around, the life-giving earth burned and roared. Vast forests crackled loudly in the fire. All the land boiled, and so did the streams of Oceanus and the barren sea. Hot vapor wrapped the earthbound Titans, and an immense flame reached the bright upper air. The dazzling glare of thunderbolt and lightning blinded their eyes, strong as they were.

An astonishing heat filled Chaos. To see it with your own eyes and hear it with your own ears was just as though Gaia, the Earth, and wide Ouranos, the Sky above, were crashing together. Such a mighty roar would rise if Gaia were collapsing and Ouranos came crashing down from high above. So great was the crash as the gods met in battle.

The winds roared amid the shaking and dust, the thunder and lightning and smoking thunderbolts—the weapons of great Zeus. They carried cries and shouts between the two armies. An immense uproar rose from that dreadful conflict, and the gods’ deeds revealed their strength.
