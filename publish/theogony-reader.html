The Muses dance on Helicon

1 Let us begin our song with the Muses of Helicon, 2 who live on Helicon, the great and sacred mountain, 3 and on soft feet, around the violet-dark spring 4 and the altar of mighty Zeus, son of Cronus, they dance. 5 After washing their tender skin in the waters of Permessus, 6 or the Horse’s Spring, or the sacred river Olmeius, 7 they form their dances on the highest slopes of Helicon, 8 beautiful, lovely dances, their feet moving with vigor.

The Muses wash in sacred waters and dance on Mount Helicon.

The Muses call Hesiod to sing

22 These Muses once taught Hesiod beautiful song 23 as he tended lambs below sacred Helicon. 24 These were the first words the goddesses spoke to me, 25 the Muses of Olympus, daughters of Zeus who bears the aegis: 26 “Shepherds who live in the fields, shameful creatures, nothing but bellies! 27 We know how to tell many lies that sound like the truth; 28 we also know, when we choose, how to speak the truth.” 29 So spoke great Zeus’s daughters, sure and skillful with words. 30 They gave me a staff, a branch of flourishing laurel 31 they had plucked, a wondrous sight, and breathed into me a voice 32 divine, so I could sing of things to come and things long past. 33 They told me to praise the family of blessed gods who live forever, 34 and always to sing of the Muses themselves, first and last.

The Muses speak sharply to Hesiod, then give him a staff and the power to sing about the gods.

What the Muses sing

35 But why all this talk of oak or rock? 36 Come, let us begin with the Muses, who sing to their father Zeus 37 and delight his mighty mind within Olympus, 38 telling of what is, what will be, and what was before, 39 their voices joining as one. Their tireless song flows 40 sweetly from their mouths, and their father’s halls laugh, 41 the halls of thundering Zeus, as the goddesses’ lily-like voices 42 spread. The peak of snowy Olympus echoes, 43 and so do the homes of the immortal gods. Raising their deathless voices, 44 the Muses first praise in song the honored family of gods 45 from the beginning, those born to Gaia the Earth and wide Uranus the Sky, 46 and the gods descended from them, the givers of good things. 47 Next they sing of Zeus, father of gods and humans, 48 both as they begin their song and as they bring it to an end, 49 telling how far he surpasses the gods, how greatest he is in power. 50 Then, singing of the human race and the mighty Giants, 51 they delight Zeus’s mind within Olympus, 52 the Muses of Olympus, daughters of Zeus who bears the aegis.

The Muses fill Olympus with song about the gods’ beginnings, Zeus’s power, humans, and Giants.

The Muses on Olympus

63 There the Muses have shining dancing grounds and beautiful homes. 64 Beside them the Graces and Himeros, Desire, have their homes 65 amid celebrations. Their gift to rulers

80 For Calliope also walks beside respected kings. 81 Whichever king the daughters of mighty Zeus choose to honor 82 and watch over at birth, among the kings sustained by Zeus, 83 they pour sweet dew upon his tongue, 84 and gentle words flow from his mouth. The people 85 all look to him as he settles disputes 86 with fair judgments. Speaking with confidence, 87 he can swiftly and skillfully end even a bitter quarrel. 88 For this is why kings are wise: for people 89 who have been wronged, they set matters right in the assembly 90 with ease, winning them over with gentle words. 91 As such a king walks through the gathering, people greet him like a god 92 with warm respect, and he stands out among the crowd. 93 Such is the Muses’ sacred gift to human beings.

Their gift to singers

94 For from the Muses and Apollo, who shoots from afar, 95 come singers and lyre players on earth, 96 and from Zeus come kings. Earth plans to stop Sky

154 For all the children born to Earth and Sky 155 were the most fearsome of children, and their father hated them 156 from the start. As soon as each was born, 157 he hid them all away and let none come into the light, 158 deep in a hollow inside Earth. He delighted in this cruel work, 159 did Sky, while huge Earth groaned within, 160 cramped by the burden, and thought up a cunning, harmful plan. 161 Quickly she created gray adamant, a substance of great hardness, 162 and made a great sickle, then explained her plan to her dear children. 163 With an aching heart, she spoke to give them courage: 164 “Children of mine and of a reckless father, if you are willing 165 to listen to me, we could make your father pay for his cruel abuse. 166 He was the first to plot these shameful deeds.” 167 So she spoke. Fear seized them all, and none of them 168 said a word. But great Cronus, with his crooked schemes, took courage 169 and answered his loving mother: 170 “Mother, I promise I can carry out this 171 deed, for I care nothing for our father 172 with his shameful name. He was the first to plot these shameful deeds.” 173 So he spoke, and huge Earth's heart filled with joy.

Sky traps his children inside Earth. In pain, she makes a sickle and asks them to help punish him. Only Cronus agrees, and Earth is delighted.

First she drew near holy Cythera, 193 then from there she reached Cyprus, surrounded by the sea. 194 Out stepped a beautiful goddess, worthy of reverence, and grass 195 grew beneath her slender feet.
